Why this matters

Phishing targets people, not just technology.

Attackers often create a sense of urgency, impersonate trusted organizations, or offer attractive rewards. A careful pause can prevent account compromise, data loss, and financial damage.

✓

Core habit

Stop, inspect, verify.

  • Do not click under pressure.
  • Check the sender and destination.
  • Verify requests through a trusted channel.

MODULE 01

What is phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering technique in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick someone into revealing information, opening a malicious file, or visiting a harmful website.

01

Email phishing

Fraudulent emails imitate banks, delivery companies, employers, or online services.

02

Spear phishing

Highly targeted messages use personal or organizational details to appear credible.

03

Smishing & vishing

Smishing uses SMS messages; vishing uses voice calls to create trust or urgency.

Remember: A message can look professional and still be malicious. Branding, logos, and familiar names are not proof of authenticity.

MODULE 02

Spot the phish

Review the examples below. These are fictional training samples. Do not interact with real suspicious links or enter credentials into unknown pages.

SUSPICIOUSExample 01

Urgent account verification required

From: account-security@secure-alerts.example

Message: Your account will be suspended in 30 minutes. Confirm your password immediately to avoid losing access.

Red flags: Artificial deadline, password request, unfamiliar sender domain.
SUSPICIOUSExample 02

You have won a reward

From: rewards@unknown-prize.example

Message: You were selected for a reward. Pay a small processing fee and provide your card details to claim it.

Red flags: Unexpected prize, payment demand, sensitive-data request.
SUSPICIOUSExample 03

Invoice attached

From: billing@vendor-check.example

Message: Please open the attached invoice immediately. Failure to respond will result in legal action.

Red flags: Unexpected attachment, intimidation, no known business context.
VERIFY FIRSTExample 04

Password reset notification

From: A service you actually use

Message: A password reset was requested. You did not request it.

Safe response: Do not use the email button. Open the official app or website manually and review account activity.

MODULE 03

Recognizing fake websites

Fraudulent websites often copy the appearance of legitimate services. Inspect the destination before entering information.

Inspect the address

  • Look for misspellings and extra words in the domain.
  • Be cautious with unusual top-level domains or shortened links.
  • HTTPS encrypts traffic but does not prove a website is legitimate.
  • Navigate manually using a known bookmark or official search result.

Watch for manipulation

  • Login pages demanding unnecessary information.
  • Pop-ups claiming your device is infected.
  • Pressure to disable security tools or install unknown software.
  • Requests for passwords, one-time codes, or payment details.

MODULE 04

Social engineering tactics

Social engineering exploits human behavior. The attacker wants you to act emotionally instead of verifying the request.

⏱

Urgency

"Act now or your account will be closed."

♙

Authority

"This is your manager. Send the file immediately."

⚠

Fear

"Your payment failed and legal action is pending."

◈

Curiosity

"See the confidential document about you."

🎁

Reward

"Claim your exclusive gift before it expires."

♧

Familiarity

Using a copied logo, name, signature, or conversation style.

MODULE 05

Safety checklist

Use this checklist whenever a message asks you to click, open, pay, download, or share information.

Best practice: When uncertain, do not respond to the suspicious message. Contact the organization using a trusted phone number, official app, or manually typed website.
